Business Efficiency Checklist

15 places your company may be losing time, missing follow-ups, or creating confusion.

Your business may not have a people problem. It may have a visibility problem. Use this checklist to find where customers, jobs, approvals, employee tasks, training, and daily updates are getting scattered.

How to use this

Count every item that feels familiar.

If you check 3 or more, your team may need a cleaner workflow. If you check 6 or more, the owner or manager has probably become the dashboard.

A Workflow Review looks at the checked items and maps where a simple command center, custom dashboard, employee portal, or better website flow could help.

Score My Workflow

Customer follow-ups live in email, texts, or memory.

The next step gets missed because there is no single place showing who needs attention.

Open jobs are tracked in a spreadsheet that only one or two people understand.

Spreadsheets can work early, but they break down when the team grows and status changes quickly.

Managers have to ask for updates more than once a day.

When the team constantly asks “where are we at with this,” visibility is missing.

Employee tasks are assigned through text threads.

Text messages are good for quick communication, but they are a poor system of record.

Approvals are buried in email.

Work submissions, payroll approvals, manager sign-offs, and exceptions need clear status and history.

Payroll visibility depends on manual review.

Hours, job counts, rates, missing information, and approved amounts should be easier to see.

Training and certifications are hard to report on.

Owners need to know who is complete, who is behind, and what still needs sign-off.

Work orders or service requests arrive from too many places.

Email, phone calls, forms, texts, and verbal requests create gaps unless they land in one queue.

Customers wait because the owner is chasing internal status.

Faster response usually starts with better internal visibility.

Reports require copying data from multiple tools.

If weekly reporting means assembling spreadsheets by hand, the workflow is probably too scattered.

Your website does not match how your business actually operates.

The website should guide people toward the right request, workflow, or next step instead of only saying “contact us.”

Important notes are stored in someone’s head.

A growing business needs a system that keeps the context, not just the person who remembers it.

There is no clear owner for overdue work.

Every important item should have a person responsible for the next action.

Employees use different tools for the same process.

Disconnected tools make it harder to know what is accurate and current.

The owner has become the system.

What this usually means

The answer is not always more software. It is one clear place to see what matters.

A focused command center can track customers, employee tasks, work submissions, approvals, jobs, training, reports, and follow-ups without forcing the team into bloated enterprise software.
